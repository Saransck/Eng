### Part 2 Grammar

#### Focus 1: Phrasal Verbs *(โน้ต: กริยาวลี 2-3 คำ)*

A phrasal verb acts as a normal verb, but it is made up of a verb and a particle or two particles. The particle is an adverb or a preposition that can combine with a verb to form the phrasal verb. There are three forms of the phrasal verb: 1) a verb + a preposition; 2) a verb + an adverb; and 3) a verb + two prepositions. The phrasal verb functions as a new verb for it has a meaning that is independent from its original word. Here are two types of the phrasal verbs.

##### 1. Non-separable phrasal verbs
The verb and the particle of a phrasal verb cannot be separate such as *look after, come across, get over, look into, look forward to,* and *come up with*.

* **Examples:**
  * My parents <u>look after</u> my baby when I am at work.
    *(look after = to take care of someone or something)*
  * We will <u>look into</u> the problem immediately.
    *(look into = to examine the facts about something)*

  * The party is tonight. The children are <u>looking forward to</u> it.
    *(look forward to = to be pleased that something is going to happen)*

##### 2. Separable phrasal verbs
The verb and the particle of a phrasal verb can be separate such as *call off, bring up, fill in, pick up,* and *turn down*.

* **Examples:**
  * The manager <u>called</u> the meeting <u>off</u>. *(โน้ต: ยกเลิก)*
    *(call off = to decide that a planned action will not happen)*
  * My parent will <u>pick</u> me <u>up</u> from the school at 4 p.m.
    *(pick up = to get someone from somewhere)*

*Sources: British Council (2024, April 12); grammarly (2024, April 12)*

#### Focus 2: Meeting Plans

##### 1. Present simple
* **Form:** $\text{Subject} + \text{verb } 1 \dots$
* The present simple is used for a future event that is <u>scheduled or timetabled</u> *(โน้ต: ตารางเวลาที่กำหนดไว้แน่นอน)*.
* **Examples:**
  * The meeting starts at 9.30 a.m.
  * The flight to Japan leaves at 5 p.m.

##### 2. Present continuous
* **Form:** $\text{Subject} + \text{be (is, am, are)} + \text{verb-ing} \dots$
* The present continuous is used for an arrangement made in the <u>near future</u>. The arrangement has a fixed time and/or place.
* **Examples:**
  * I am flying to Hong Kong tomorrow morning.
  * We are having a meeting at 3 this afternoon.

##### 3. Future Simple
* **Form:** $\text{Subject} + \text{will} + \text{verb } 1 \dots$
* The future simple is used to express two things.
  * **3.1. Future prediction**
    * **Examples:**
      * We think the cost of transportation will increase.
      * If the manager doesn't feel better, he will cancel the meeting.
      * Unless Sue has experiences, she won't get a job.
  * **3.2. Spontaneous decision, offer, or promise**
    * **Examples:**
      * The printer is out of paper. I will get you some papers.
      * Mark is busy. I will receive the phone call for him.
      * Mr. Ben wants to see a report. I will finish the report tomorrow.
      * Everyone is having a meeting. I will take care of the customers.

**Remarks:** The following adverbs can be used to show the certainty of actions.

| Degree of Certainty | stronger $\longleftarrow$ | $\longrightarrow$ | weaker |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Positive Adverb** | definitely | probably | perhaps/maybe |
| **Negative Adverb** | definitely not | probably not | perhaps not/maybe not |

* **Examples:**
  * We think the cost of transportation will <u>definitely</u> increase.
  * Unless Sue has experiences, she <u>probably</u> won't get a job.
  * Mr. Ben wants to see a report. <u>Maybe</u> I will finish the report tomorrow.

##### 4. Be going to
* **Form:** $\text{Subject} + \text{be (is, am, are)} + \text{going to} + \text{verb } 1 \dots$
* "Be going to" is used for a plan that we have already decided. It emphasizes on an intention to do something.
* **Examples:**
  * Sue is going to attend the conference next week.
  * He is going to present our new product.
  * I am going to take the minutes of the meeting.
  * They are going to do the project.

*Sources: Business Essentials B1 (2019); test-english (2024, April 10)*

### Part 3 Reading: Job Satisfaction

**Read the passage and do the exercises that follow.**

#### Job Satisfaction

Job satisfaction statistics have been an important tool for employers in looking for and understanding their employees' work happiness. The following are statistics on American job satisfaction and dissatisfaction which can be a practical advice to American employers who want to ensure a positive work environment for their employees.

##### What affects employees' job satisfaction?
There are several factors affecting employees' job satisfaction.
* Sixty percent of workers think that <u>coworkers</u> have the most influence on their job happiness. The report in 2019 indicated that two in three employees consider the people whom they work with have the greatest contribution to job satisfaction.
* Nearly $57\%$ of workers globally are satisfied with their employment. According to the research by GoodFirms, about $50\%$ of employees are satisfied with their <u>positions</u> while about $30\%$ are not. Thirteen percent of employees are unsure or neutral to their present jobs.
* Reporting in the survey by GoodFirms, most respondents ($89.05\%$) expect the three prioritized <u>workplace benefits</u> which are <u>annual raises</u> ($81.10\%$), <u>five working days a week</u> ($75.33\%$), and <u>flexible timings</u> ($74.54\%$).
* Exactly $70.87\%$ of employees consider the importance of <u>fair treatment</u> in a workplace, including having <u>work related to their interests</u> ($71.40\%$), achieving a <u>healthy work/life balance</u> ($70.60\%$), and having a <u>friendly boss</u> ($69.53\%$). *(โน้ต: สวัสดิการ / สิ่งจูงใจ)*
* According to the Society of Human Resource Management, $72.0\%$ of employees rank <u>respect</u> within a workplace as the most important factor to job satisfaction.

##### What affects employees' job dissatisfaction?
Along with job satisfaction, employers also need to know what cause employees' dissatisfaction in job.
* Seventy-nine percent of employees reported feeling <u>undervalued</u> as a main reason to quit their jobs. Sixty-five percent of employees noted their manager's failure to recognize their hard work even once in 12 months.
* Gallup research revealed that only $34.0\%$ of US employees feel <u>engaged</u> in a workplace. Most of them feel that their jobs aren't fulfilling enough.

* Although money is an important factor, HR Daily Advisor found that only $12.0\%$ of employees consider leaving a job due to <u>pay grade</u> alone.
* Contrarily, the statistics show that $75.0\%$ of employees quit their jobs due to managerial <u>disagreements</u>. Those who enjoy a close relationship with management team are likely to be more productive and remain in their roles.
* In a recent LinkedIn survey, $73.0\%$ of employees would stay with a company if there were more <u>skill-building opportunities</u> on offer.

##### How is age influenced to job satisfaction?
Recent job satisfaction statistics have shown difference in the priorities and happiness of employees across different age ranges.
* Thirty-one percent of workers who are 18- to 34- years old are satisfied with their jobs while $40.0\%$ of <u>those</u> 35 and older report complete satisfaction with their present jobs.
* Full-time workers ($34.0\%$) are more satisfied with jobs than part-time workers ($23.0\%$).
* More companies ($56.0\%$) have updated their policies to appeal to a multi-generational workforce such as baby boomers, Gen-X, Gen-Y, and Gen-Z workers.
* Workers who are 18- to 34- years old most likely prioritize pay rates and progression opportunities as important factors to job satisfaction. A recent CNBC/Survey Monkey research found $35.0\%$ of workers cited job meaningfulness as crucial to job satisfaction.
* Likewise, a Pew Research survey indicated $59.0\%$ of older workers prioritize job security as more important than salary whereas workers between 18- and 24-years old value a higher salary over job security.
* Millennials are the largest generation of US workers. They are at least three times more likely to quit their jobs than other generations. Forty-three percent reported that they will change jobs in the next two years if they get more money and opportunities.

##### What are the impacts of job dissatisfaction?
Although job satisfaction continues to grow, employers don't care enough or fail to understand the <u>severity</u> of the problem. Let's see what the study found out about the results of ongoing job dissatisfaction.
* As many as $52.0\%$ of American workforce are <u>uninspired and undermotivated</u>. Majority of these employees ($67.0\%$) believe that things have gotten worse since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic.
* A Bankrate survey from August 2021 revealed that $55.0\%$ of US workers are likely to look for a new job <u>within the next 12 months</u>.
* Companies that overlook employee satisfaction <u>cost</u> *(โน้ต: ทำให้เสีย)* the US as much as $\$450\text{--}\$550$ billion each year due to increased <u>turnover</u> *(โน้ต: อัตราการลาออก)* and employment drives, poorer employee productivity, and a general lack of effort on their part.
* Research by Edelman Trust Barometer found that only $48.0\%$ of non-managerial staff trust their companies. This makes every day work and overall company growth harder since mistrust leads to lower work engagement, unhappiness in the workplace, and overall decreased productivity.
